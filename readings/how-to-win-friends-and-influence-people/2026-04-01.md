## Chapter 1 — If You Want to Gather Honey, Don't Kick the Beehive

- Criminals rarely see themselves as bad — they rationalize, explain, justify.
- Criticism is foolish. 99/100 times people don't criticize themselves no matter how wrong they are.
- People defend and self-impose under pressure. Forgive rather than bark — they already feel bad.
- Instead of "Do this!", ask "What can we do better?"
- We thirst for approval and dread condemnation. Reward, don't punish.
- Check yourself first. Work on yourself before you work on others.

## Chapter 2 — The Big Secret in Dealing with People

- The one thing: what do THEY want?
- The deepest craving in human nature is the craving to be appreciated.
- People may go to extremes — even insanity — to find the feeling of importance denied them in real life.
- People work harder under sincere appreciation than under criticism.
- Flattery ≠ appreciation. Be sincere and thoughtful.
- Give genuine appreciation, not hollow compliments.

## Chapter 3

Core idea: to get someone to do what you want, you first have to understand it from their perspective. Most people only look at things from their own angle. Everything changes when you flip that.

Carried this into parenting today — consciously tried to see things from the kids' point of view instead of reacting from mine. Noticeable difference.
