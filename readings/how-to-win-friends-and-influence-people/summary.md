# How to Win Friends and Influence People
*Dale Carnegie*

**Status:** In progress
**Format:** Physical / Reading

---

## What kind of book is this

A practical guide to human relationships — less about manipulation, more about genuinely understanding people. The core insight is that most conflict and friction in life comes from people being too focused on their own perspective to ever really see anyone else's. Carnegie is old-school in voice but the principles are timeless. The value is in applying it in real time, not just reading it.

---

## Chapter Summaries

### Ch 1 — Don't Criticize

**Main Idea:** Criticism almost never produces change — people rationalize and defend no matter how wrong they are, so the move is to ask better questions and check yourself first before trying to change anyone else.

- Criminals rarely see themselves as bad — they rationalize, explain, justify
- 99/100 times people don't criticize themselves no matter how wrong they are
- People defend and dig in under pressure — forgive rather than attack, they often already feel bad
- Instead of "do this!" ask "what can we do better?"
- We thirst for approval and dread condemnation — reward, don't punish
- Check yourself first; work on yourself before you try to work on others

> **Connection:** "Check yourself first" maps directly to the engineering instinct — grill yourself on the design before touching code. The `/grill-me` command exists for exactly this reason.

---

### Ch 2 — The Big Secret in Dealing with People

**Main Idea:** The deepest craving in human nature is to feel genuinely appreciated — not flattered, but actually seen — and people will go to extraordinary lengths to get that feeling, so give it sincerely and it changes everything.

- The one thing: what do THEY want?
- The deepest craving in human nature is the craving to be appreciated
- People may go to extremes — even destructive ones — to find the feeling of importance denied them in real life
- People work harder and go further under sincere appreciation than under any amount of pressure
- Flattery ≠ appreciation — be sincere and thoughtful, not hollow
- Give genuine appreciation; it requires actually seeing the person

> **Connection:** Where in your everyday life are you giving genuine appreciation vs. going through the motions? With the kids especially — do they feel seen, or just managed?

---

### Ch 3 — See It From Their Angle First

**Main Idea:** To get anyone to do anything, you first have to understand what they actually want — most people never flip the perspective, and the ones who can do it genuinely have an enormous advantage in every relationship.

- To get someone to do something, understand it from their perspective first
- Most people only look at things from their own angle — this is the default and it's costly
- Everything changes when you genuinely flip to the other side
- *"It's a miracle what can happen when you look at things from someone else's point of view."*

> **Connection:** Applied this directly in parenting — consciously tried to see things from the kids' point of view instead of reacting from mine. Noticeable difference, same day.

## Connections & Application

**Parenting is the live lab for this book.** Ch 3 was applied the same day it was read and it worked. The principles here are easiest to see with kids because the power dynamic is so obvious — they can't push back the way adults can, so if something isn't working, it's almost always because you're operating from your own perspective instead of theirs.

**The feedback loop makes it real.** Section 2, Ch 1 was applied five seconds after reading — showed genuine interest with Hunter, asked outright how it made her feel, got live confirmation it worked. Read → apply → test → confirm. This is how Carnegie's principles stop being theory. The sincerity wasn't forced — it was already there; the book just gave it language.

**The appreciation principle (Ch 2) is worth turning into a practice.** Not a vague intention — a literal question to ask at the end of the day: did I give any genuine appreciation today, or was it all feedback and correction? With the kids, with yourself, with anyone. The craving is real and universal.

**"Check yourself first" (Ch 1) applies everywhere.** Before getting frustrated at someone, before giving feedback, before pushing back on an idea — run the self-check. Is this mine to criticize, or mine to own first?

**The perspective flip (Ch 3) also applies to learning.** When a concept isn't clicking, the instinct is to blame the explanation. But flipping the question to "what is this trying to solve, from the designer's perspective?" often breaks the logjam.

---

### Section 2, Ch 1 — Do This and You'll Be Welcome Anywhere

**Main Idea:** You can make more friends in two months by becoming genuinely interested in other people than in two years by trying to get people interested in you. Genuine interest — not performed, not strategic — is the whole game.

- Genuine interest in people is more powerful than any social tactic
- The individual not interested in his fellow men has the greatest difficulties in life — all human failures spring from among such people
- All of us like people who admire us
- Put yourself out for others — time, energy, selflessness, thoughtfulness
- Greet people with animation and enthusiasm
- Genuine interest may get you what you want without asking
- Acknowledging someone's interest *outside* the original conversation is enormous (e.g., days later: "I think that bird you'd never seen before is outside your window right now")
- An enthusiastic "Hi" when you pick up the phone goes a long way
- Even small interests spark big opportunities

> **Connection:** The difference between genuine and performed interest is the same distinction Carnegie made with appreciation vs. flattery in Ch. 2. The through-line in this book: sincerity is not optional — people feel the difference.
