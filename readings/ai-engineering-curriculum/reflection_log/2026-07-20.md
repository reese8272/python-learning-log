## What was taught (current pattern)

**The 2026 steering ladder** (the real unit — not "zero vs few shot"):

1. **Zero-shot + clear/direct instructions** — the default. Docs: try direct instruction first; golden rule = "if a minimal-context colleague would be confused, Claude will be." 2026 addition: give the *why* behind rules ("read aloud by TTS, so no ellipses") — the model generalizes from motivation.
2. **Few-shot** — for *fuzzy* steering (tone, judgment boundaries, edge cases). Still endorsed: "one of the most reliable ways to steer output format, tone, and structure." Current spec: **3–5 examples**, `<example>`/`<examples>` tags, **diverse** — examples teach everything they contain including accidental patterns (uniform labels → silent bias).
3. **Structured Outputs** — when shape must be *guaranteed*. Schema enforces; examples nudge. Guarantees **shape, not content quality**. This corrects the 2024 answer "use examples for specific formats."

Cost logic: examples are re-sent every call (§1.1 stateless model) — never pay for them to do a job schema enforcement does free.

**Role prompting** (orthogonal): one-sentence prior-shift in the **top-level `system` param** → part of the cacheable prefix (render order tools→system→messages, §1.2) → written once, steers every turn ~free. Genuinely changes tone/vocabulary AND scope/judgment defaults. Does **not** add knowledge or accuracy.

**⚠ Currency landmine confirmed live:** aggressive prompting ("CRITICAL: You MUST…", "If in doubt, always…") now **overtriggers** on 4.5/4.6+ — models follow the system prompt closely, so 2023-era overpowering language causes over-eager behavior. Fix: plain scoped sentences ("Use this tool when…"). A course recording teaches the aggressive style as a technique; it's now an anti-pattern.
