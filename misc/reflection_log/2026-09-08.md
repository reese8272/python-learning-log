# 2026-09-08 — Retirement note: the AI YouTube Editor

*Written at `/audit` #1, per the no-silent-abandonment rule. Decision was Reese's; the note is the receipt.*

---

## The 100 words

The Editor was scoped on 08-04 to a real spine — upload → good shorts → titles/captions → download — with a real acceptance test (*would the lead actually post these?*) and a real date, NFL Week 1. The scope was right. The date was chosen when the Editor was the only thing competing for attention.

Then a Cognizant project turned genuinely good, Ian started feeding paid contract work, and the record from 35 days is unambiguous: **zero recorded touches, including a twelve-day stretch with enough energy for a full system rewrite.** That is not a scheduling failure. It is an answer.

Shelved, said out loud, on 09-08 — two days before the date it would otherwise have quietly slipped past.

---

## Why this is a shelf and not a failure

Three things are worth keeping straight, because the difference between them is the whole reason this note exists.

**1. The scoping work was good and it survives.** The spine, the acceptance test, and the cut list in `agenda.md` are still the right answers to *"what would v1 be?"* Nothing about the shelf says they were wrong. If this comes back, it comes back with the 08-04 scoping intact and doesn't need redoing.

**2. The date was the mistake, not the project.** 08-04's "ship by kickoff" was written against ~18 usable days on a summer cadence, with the Editor as the sole commitment. By 08-27 there were three lanes. The system's own documented finding is that it does not carry three full-weight commitments at once — that's the over-launch signature, recorded at 07-07, 07-10, 07-20, and 07-27. Keeping the date would have been the fourth time ignoring it.

**3. The honest test was time-with-energy, and it was run.** Between 08-27 and 09-08 there was clearly capacity — a 235-line researched scheduler spec came out of it. None of that capacity went to the Editor. When something loses a fair fight for attention over 35 days, moving its deadline is not a plan; it's a way of not deciding.

---

## What the shelf costs, stated plainly

**The lead.** Owed a message since 08-10 — 29 days as of today. That message is now the shelf message, and this is the part that actually matters:

> A stated delay costs nothing; silence costs the relationship.

Telling someone honestly that you're not building it preserves the relationship. Twenty-nine days of silence followed by a *third* date would not. **This is the five-minute action that closes both this decision and the oldest open item in the system.**

---

## Conditions for it coming back

Not "someday." Written now so the answer isn't reconstructed later under motivation:

1. **A lane exists for it.** The Business lane's only defined client work is currently a question mark next to Ian's name. The Editor comes back when the Business lane is *moving*, not as the thing meant to start it moving.
2. **Someone is waiting.** The acceptance test was always *"would he post these?"* — one interested human. If the lead (or another) actively asks, that's a different situation and a real signal.
3. **At an `/audit`, never on a motivated night.** Standing rule: plans that appear after 10pm go to the next check-in.

---

## The thing worth not losing

The 08-04 read on where the value sat — *clip selection quality on long-form video is ~80% of the value and the only genuinely hard problem; upload/download are plumbing; titles and captions are garnish* — is a good piece of product judgment and it was reached by noticing a misallocation in his own nine-item list. That judgment is portable. It belongs to him regardless of whether this product ever ships.

**CFO Agent stays demoable** (177 tests, Redis-backed LangGraph checkpointing, circulated unprompted by a recruiter on 07-10). Shelving the Editor is not shelving the products line.
