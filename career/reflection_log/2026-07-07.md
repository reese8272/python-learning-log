## The mini-curriculum (proposed map for this domain)
1. **Scope of impact** — mindset shift "my code" → "the team's outcome" *(taught today)*
2. Communicating to reduce ambiguity — BLUF, audience-tuning, design-doc muscle
3. Disagreeing well — strong opinions loosely held, disagree-and-commit
4. Code review as a relationship — teach not gatekeep; receive without ego
5. Making your work legible — valued without gross self-promotion
6. Glue work & mentorship — the invisible senior-making work, without the martyr trap

## Unit 1 — Scope of Impact (taught)

**Mechanism — impact is measured differently per level:**
- Junior: "did I finish the task I was given?"
- Mid-level: "did I deliver my project well, on my own?" (strong technician, own scope)
- Senior: **the delta you create in the people + systems around you** — "how much better did everyone get because I was here?" Individual output stops being the thing measured. Can't out-code your way to senior; it's a different skill bolted on top.

**The concrete behavioral model (his stated gap — "I don't know what I'm looking for in a senior"):** what a senior *does* that makes people defer —
1. **Kills ambiguity** — turns a stuck, foggy project into a decision. (Reilly: senior's job is making the messy ambiguous thing manageable for others.)
2. **Concrete over safe** — states a clear position even at risk of being wrong. **"Wrong gets corrected. Vague sticks around." (Tanya Reilly.)** Decisive-and-correctable beats safe-and-useless.
3. **Multiplies through review + teaching** — reviews and explains the *why*, so the class of bug never recurs (compounding, not linear).
4. **Questions the requirement** — optimizes the outcome/business goal, not just the ticket as written.
5. **Does glue work** — the connective tissue nobody else writes (Reilly named it).

**Failure modes (Tier-1 bar) — two opposite ways it breaks:**
- **Hoarder/hero (stays mid-level):** takes all hard problems solo → becomes a bottleneck + diminisher; nobody else grows. The ceiling for strong mid-levels.
- **Martyr (glue trap):** does so much invisible multiplier work they stop shipping visible things → glue work is often *non-promotable*, never credited. Fix: unstick it *enough*, hand it back, keep own signal legible. (= Units 5 & 6.)
- The real skill is the **balance**: multiply others AND stay visible.

**Tie to him:** these behaviors are available at 1yr — don't wait for the title. Interview tell: junior says "I built X"; senior-track says "team was stuck on Y, I unblocked with Z, chose A over B because…". His **Judgment Log ("why THIS over THAT") is already this muscle** — senior communication is externalizing that reasoning so others can build on it.
