# Soft Skills & Corporate Navigation Guide

**Target:** Navigate corporations effectively, build reputation deliberately, develop executive presence  
**Reading block:** 15–20 minutes/day — rotate through the list in order, one book at a time  
**Mastery standard:** Can I use this in the next conversation I have?

---

## The Three Layers

Technical skill gets you in the room. These three layers determine what happens once you're there.

### 1. Corporate Navigation — how power and decisions actually work
The org chart is a lie. Decisions move through relationships, reputation, and who's in the room.
- Make your boss look good — consistently. That's not politics, that's leverage.
- Find the informal decision-makers. They're usually not the highest title.
- Volunteer for high-visibility projects even when it's outside your lane.
- Write things up and share them — recaps, summaries, findings. Quiet work is invisible work.
- Solve problems before they're asked to be solved.

### 2. Reputation — being known for something specific
You can't be known for everything early. Own one thing, then expand.
- Your one thing: AI/agentic systems + enterprise automation. Own it.
- Under-promise, over-deliver — every single time.
- Be specific about your wins. "I built X that reduced Y by Z%" — not "I worked on the AI project."
- Be the person who says "I'll figure it out" not "that's not my job."

### 3. Executive Presence — how you look, act, sound
The most learnable of the three. The gap most technical people never close.
- Speak less, say more. Filling silence signals insecurity.
- Lead with the conclusion, then the reasoning. Not the other way around.
- Ask questions that show you've thought deeply — not ones that show you're catching up.
- Own mistakes fast and publicly. It builds trust faster than being right all the time.
- Carry yourself like the next level. People promote who they can already picture in the role.
