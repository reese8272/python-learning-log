# 2026-07-27 — Making learning addicting: engineer the reward side of the loop

Came out of asking "how do I make learning addicting? Or doing the hard things addicting?" — answered against SYSTEM.md rather than from scratch.

## The core insight

You can't make hard things addicting in the TikTok sense, and the system already knows that — it's built on cues, not cravings. But everything addictive runs the same four-step circuit: **cue → craving → action → reward**, where the reward is *fast, variable, and visible*. The Cue Map is world-class on cue and action. The part the system barely engineers is the **reward side** — and that's exactly where "addicting" lives. Slot machines aren't addictive because the lever is easy to pull; they're addictive because the payoff is immediate and unpredictable. Learning's natural payoff is neither. So the work is compressing the reward into the session itself.

## The five levers (mapped to what already exists)

1. **Make the reward arrive in minutes, not months.** "Build before you bank" is secretly the most addictive rule in the system — running code is a slot machine: write, run, it breaks or works, dopamine either way, pull again. Practical move: bias every `/learn` and `/sharpen` toward code executing *by minute 10*, not as end-of-session homework. Reading for 40 minutes then coding is study; coding at minute 10 is a game.
2. **Exploit variable reward — `/sharpen` already is one.** Defending cold with real uncertainty is the variable-reward mechanic. What kills it is miscalibration: too easy → boredom, too hard → the judgment-exposure spiral. Target: "just past the edge of what I can do." If sessions feel reliably survivable or reliably brutal, that's the dial.
3. **End on a cliffhanger, not a clean finish.** Free upgrade to the stopped-at line. Hemingway's trick: stop *mid-problem*, deliberately, so the open loop itches (Zeigarnik effect — unfinished tasks occupy the brain rent-free). Stop three lines into the next thing: "Stopped at: [X], and I already know the next move is [Y]." Re-entry tomorrow starts with an easy win you're craving instead of a cold start.
4. **Make progress visible like an RPG stat screen.** Streaks on the Card and skill levels in CAREER_LOG are already leveling infrastructure — the gap is not *looking* at them as a reward. A 30-second block-close ritual (glance at the streak, glance at what leveled up) is the "XP gained" screen. Silly-sounding; it's the exact mechanic every game uses to make grinding feel good.
5. **Kill the competition — dopamine is comparative.** Nothing hard out-rewards a phone in the same room; the brain prices rewards relative to the best available alternative. Phone-out-of-the-room isn't just anti-distraction — it's what makes a moderately-rewarding hard thing the *most* rewarding thing available. Learning gets addictive partly by being the best game in the room.

## The honest caveats

- **Chemistry gates everything.** Craving is a dopamine phenomenon; cardio-before-peak and sleep aren't adjacent to this question, they *are* it (Layer 1).
- **Watch the frame.** "Make it addicting" is motivation-surge language, and over-launch is the documented collapse signature. The real mechanism is **learned industriousness**: after enough repetitions where effort reliably pays off fast, the brain tags effort itself as reward-predicting and the pull becomes real. That takes weeks of the loop closing — which is what the cue architecture buys time to reach. The system's bet was never "want it every day"; it's "show up until wanting shows up."

## Cheapest installs (tweaks to existing rules, not new bricks)

- Cliffhanger stopped-at line (stop mid-problem, name the next move).
- Code executing by minute 10 in every `/learn` / `/sharpen`.
